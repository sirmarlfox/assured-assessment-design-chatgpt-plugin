## Appendix B: Source registry and baseline snapshot

Status: **Read** = the builder fetched and read the page or document on 24 Nov 2026. **Seen** = appeared only in search results; verify before relying on it. Always run Step 0 and prefer anything newer.

**Frameworks**
- CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0, released 30 July 2024; current version at build. https://udlguidelines.cast.org (Seen in search results; release date confirmed there).
- Inner Development Guide 2025, 25 skills, five dimensions (Being, Thinking, Relating, Collaborating, Acting). Names used by the cards exist in it. The IDG framework page may still say 23 skills; the 2021 framework used different names. https://innerdevelopmentgoals.org (Read via the EUR descriptions copy and search results).
- Revised Bloom's taxonomy (Krathwohl, 2002).

**Australia: TEQSA (regulator)**
- Assessment reform for the age of artificial intelligence, 23 November 2023: two guiding principles (equip students for an AI world; trustworthy judgements need multiple, contextualised approaches across a course). Seen.
- Enacting assessment reform in a time of artificial intelligence, 24 September 2025. Read. Three pathways: (1) assure learning across the whole program, (2) assure by unit with at least one secure task per unit with identity verification, controlled conditions and a hurdle, (3) hybrid. Secure formats named include oral presentations, in-class tasks, supervised practical demonstrations and invigilated tests. It warns that unit-level security brings scheduling, supervision and documentation overhead, and that reverting to familiar secure formats may worsen inequities. It does not specifically address unsecured take-home tasks paired with in-person checkpoints; do not claim that it does.
- Assuring quality learning in a gen AI-integrated future: the role of adaptive capabilities, 24 June 2026. Third in the series; focuses on evaluative judgement, critical thinking and ethical reasoning, and offers actionable approaches. Read in full via the PDF: https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-06/assuring-quality-learning-in-a-gen-AI-integrated-future.pdf . Lead author Jason M Lodge with co-authors. Advisory, not part of TEQSA's Guidance Notes. Four adaptive capabilities (digital literacy, distributed cognition, hybrid metacognition including evaluative judgement, life-long learning). Proposes process-focused assessment pairing documentation of the learning process with final products, desirable difficulties, and gradual transfer of regulation from AI-supported to student-led. Cautions: process evidence is a partial window, not foolproof; surveillance risks; equity of access to commercial AI; secure forms should continue until better approaches emerge. It does not mention oral assessment or supervised checkpoints on take-home work; do not claim it does. https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/higher-education-good-practice-hub/gen-ai-knowledge-hub/gen-ai-teqsa-resources
- Context: TEQSA signalled a shift from an educative to a regulatory-led approach beginning in 2026 (reported by the University of Sydney). The "two-lane" label (secure Lane 1, open Lane 2) comes from the University of Sydney; some Australian universities call Lane 1 tasks "assured". Seen.

**England: Office for Students and Advance HE**
- Making AI work for students: reflections on institutional practice (OfS and Advance HE), 11 November 2026, ref OfS 2026.56. Read. Research June to September 2026, over 300 students and staff from over 120 institutions. Findings include: AI poses significant validity risks to unsupervised assessment and detection tools are unreliable; secure approaches designed to prevent AI use will often be most effective; real-world assessments that integrate AI can be positive but need careful design; students are often unclear on AI rules; AI may help neurodivergent, disabled and non-native English speakers, and AI literacy provision should reflect unequal access; no detailed assessment-specific inclusion guidance. https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/publications/making-ai-work-for-students-reflections-on-institutional-practice/
- OfS press release, 11 November 2026: the OfS will require institutions to ensure assessments are valid and reliable, which in many cases will require secure AI-free assessment; the accompanying research says closed-book exams, presentations, practical assessments and performances are likely the most valid measures. It links OfS guidance on complying with its regulation in the context of AI: https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/for-providers/quality-and-standards/artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/effective-institutional-practice/ (link seen, guidance itself not read in full: read it at Step 0 when the user is in England). Participants also warned that new AI-specific registration conditions could be counterproductive.
- Full PDF of the OfS and Advance HE report: https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/media/nf2ffugd/making-ai-work-for-students.pdf . Read (assessment content Chapter 2 pp. 16 to 24, executive summary p. 4, recommendations paras 128 to 133). Reported redesign approaches: secure assessments (in-person exams, presentations, vivas, short discussions of submitted work, practical or performance), process-based assessment with stage gates, and AI-permitted assessment (critiquing AI output, editing an AI draft); some institutions stress-test assessments with AI; barriers include exam and viva resourcing, disadvantage to disabled students, professional-body rules and wearables; most changes so far are instructions to students rather than structural redesign; all assessments must be inclusive; Bath adopted a two-lane model. Effective practice: secure assessment that prevents AI use, valid non-secure design, clear communication of permitted AI use, and no excessive reliance on AI detectors. Footnote pointers (Seen only): University of Melbourne secure assessment typology; Curtin case studies; QAA work on AI and award standards; Jisc digital assessment work.
- Advance HE, Enhancing assessment in higher education (2024): listed in a University of London resource menu; Seen only.

**Scholarly and sector anchors**
- Corbin, T., Dawson, P., & Liu, D. (2025). Talk is cheap: why structural assessment changes are needed for a time of GenAI. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education, 50(7). https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2025.2503964 . Distinguishes discursive changes (rules and instructions) from structural changes (redesigning the task). Seen via abstracts and summaries.
- Curtis, G. J. (2025). The two-lane road to hell is paved with good intentions. Higher Education Research and Development. Critiques all-or-none two-lane thinking and argues for a "middle lane" with limited AI use; cited on a TEQSA resource page. Seen.
- Droulers, M., Krautloher, A., & Shaeri, S. (2025). Interactive oral assessment: co-existence of formative and summative purposes. Teaching in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1080/13562517.2025.2549945 . Seen. Interactive oral assessments originate from Griffith University (2015) and are reported as used in over 30 universities (Times Higher Education, 11 Aug 2025). University of Sydney defines IOAs as secure assessments and distinguishes them from viva voce exams.
- Take-home plus interactive oral exam designs in engineering education (ASEE conference paper) and identity-verified oral checks (Times Higher Education) illustrate Anchor designs. Seen only; use as practitioner examples, not as evidence of effectiveness.
- Ireland: HEA guidance on generative AI and assessment (hub.teachingandlearning.ie). Seen only; check for Irish users.

When a user is outside England and Australia, search for their national regulator or quality body and say that the registry above covers only those two plus general scholarship.
